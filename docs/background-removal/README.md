# Background removal, measured against real input

Three pictures of the kind people actually upload, and what the removal makes
of them. `*-source.png` is the input, `*-before.png` is the cut-out as it was
before Stage 3, `*-after.png` is the cut-out now. The chequerboard is not part
of the picture — it shows where the cut-out is see-through.

The pictures are generated arithmetically from a fixed seed rather than
photographed, so the comparison is repeatable and nobody's photograph is in the
repository. Nothing here is generated by AI: the grain comes from a linear
congruential generator, the lighting from a radial gradient, the shapes from
arcs and béziers. Run the generator twice and the files are byte for byte the
same.

## The three

| File | What it stands for | The hard part |
|---|---|---|
| `cream-*` | A drawing on cream paper, photographed at an angle so one corner is in shadow | The paper is not white and not evenly lit |
| `texture-*` | A page under a desk lamp: strong grain, visible fibres, a bright hot-spot in one corner | The bright corner is further from the average paper colour than the drawing is |
| `object-*` | A plain object on a flat background with a soft contact shadow | The shadow fades continuously into the object it belongs to |

## What changed

| Picture | Specks before | Specks after | Separate pieces before | after | Soft edge pixels before | after |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| cream | 0 | 0 | 2 | 2 | 0 | 69 |
| texture | 7490 | 2 | 17 | 2 | 0 | 104 |
| object | 85 | 0 | 2 | 2 | 0 | 73 |

A *speck* is a connected region of 60 pixels or fewer — too small to be
something anybody drew, so it is paper grain stuck to the sticker or a pinhole
punched through it. *Separate pieces* counts regions bigger than that: two is
the right answer for all three pictures (the subject, and everything else), and
seventeen means the cut-out came apart. *Soft edge pixels* are neither fully
kept nor fully dropped, which is what a photographed edge needs and a hard
threshold cannot give.

The single worst failure was the bright corner in `texture`: a large blotch of
unremoved paper, with about 7,500 grains of it scattered around. Look at
`texture-before.png` and `texture-after.png` side by side — it is the clearest
picture of what the three changes buy.

## The three changes

**It walks down a gradient instead of measuring from one colour.** A page
photographed under a lamp is far brighter at one end, and a single averaged
paper colour with a fixed tolerance leaves the bright end behind. A pixel now
joins the background if it is close to *the pixel it spread from* as well as
broadly paper-like overall, so the flood walks down the lighting in small steps
and a real edge is a step too big to cross. `flood` gained an optional second
argument for this — the pixel a step is coming from — which `despeckle` and the
colouring fill ignore.

**It ignores grain.** Regions of 48 pixels or fewer become whatever surrounds
them. This was the big one: 7490 specks to 2.

**It cuts softly.** Where the flood stopped because one step was a little too
big — a gentle fade rather than a drawn line — the boundary pixel comes out
part-transparent in proportion. Where the step was far too big, it stays solid,
because that is an edge and not a fade.

## What was tried and taken out

A **tapering step**, tightening the further a pixel is from the paper colour,
to stop the flood following a soft shadow into the object it belongs to. It
made the shadow slightly better and the uneven lighting much worse — brightly
lit paper is itself a long way from the average paper colour, so the taper would
not let the flood reach it, and `texture` went from 502 specks to 1068 with the
bright corner breaking into scattered fragments. Reverted.

## What it still cannot do

Stated on the screen, in these words:

- Busy photographs, where there is no one background to find.
- Hair and fur — thousands of strands thinner than the cut.
- Glass and anything see-through: it can keep it or drop it, not both.
- A soft shadow touching the object, which runs smoothly into the edge and takes
  a bite out of it.
- A background that fades from one colour to a different one, rather than the
  same colour lit unevenly.

Every one of those is what the touch-up brushes are for.

## Regenerating this

The generator and the measurement live outside the repository, in the session
scratchpad, because they exist to produce these files rather than to ship. The
numbers above come from `backgroundMask(source, 14)` with the stated defaults
(`step: 6`, `softness: 5`, `speck: 48`), which `tests/colour.test.mjs` asserts,
so a change to them is a change somebody made on purpose.
