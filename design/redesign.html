WHAT THE HARBOUR KEEPS

Salt on the rail and the light going amber,
    the boats not yet in.

A woman counts the ropes she does not own
and finds the number steady.

Nothing here is kept for long —
    not the weather, not the men,
not the small change of the tide
    turning over in its sleep.

Only the wall remembers,
and the wall says little.

She walks the length of it at seven
    and again at four,

and the harbour keeps what harbours keep:
the rope, the rust, the ring,
    the year somebody carved
and no one since has asked about.
